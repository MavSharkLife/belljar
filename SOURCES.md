# Image sources and historical context

Every photograph and news image comes from a named historical archive or period newspaper collection. There is no AI-generated or stock imagery. No blood, wounds, people, or props have been generated or composited into the archival images. Pages 05, 09, and 10 use a photograph and cropped caption panel from a 1950 newspaper picture story; CSS adds restrained scanlines and contrast to fit the shared analog-horror treatment.

## Use and context

Pages 09–12 are image-only, directly loadable archive leaves with no navigation. Pages 05, 09, and 10 foreground the two patients in the *Minneapolis Morning Tribune* report: the article says a restraint was used to prevent a woman from scratching her head, and reports aides' concern that a man would scratch his eyes without a straitjacket. Neither photo shows a razor injury, and these patients' experiences should not be treated as equivalent to Esther's. Pages 11 and 12 show actual ECT sessions as historical treatment context.

## Page 13: school mental-health funding

Page 13 summarizes the [New York Times report supplied for this project](https://www.nytimes.com/2025/05/01/us/politics/trump-mental-health-grants.html). The NYT page could not be opened in the research browser, so the summary is cross-checked against contemporaneous reporting: [Associated Press, May 1, 2025](https://apnews.com/article/school-mental-health-grants-trump-biden-dei-00bec2d96371f023ac56fe3f32f3e92f) says recipients were told the roughly $1 billion in school mental-health grants would not continue after that year and describes the funding as intended to help schools hire psychologists, counselors, and other mental-health staff. [The Washington Post, May 1, 2025](https://www.washingtonpost.com/education/2025/05/01/trump-administration-cuts-school-mental-health-grants/) reported concerns about planned counseling, staffing, and training pipelines.

The page also includes the later status so the 2025 announcement is not mistaken for the current nationwide position: [a September 2026 federal court order](https://docs.justia.com/cases/federal/district-courts/washington/wawdce/2%3A2026cv02409/365421/106) granted a preliminary injunction against the Department's later termination plan for grantees covered by an earlier injunction, in a case brought by 15 states. The order does not guarantee payments; it notes grantees lacked assurance that expenses would be reimbursed after July 31, 2026. A later [AP report on a rural Kentucky district](https://apnews.com/article/trump-education-grants-rural-schools-mental-health-0a2c368ddbc9058babf304eef5534b13) described a grant-funded counselor role left unfilled after its funding ended. The page labels the possible service gap as a risk and the court status as preliminary.
